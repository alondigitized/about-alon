Retail Intelligence Pulse

Sunday, August 23 2026 · Footwear, digital commerce, marketplaces

What newly matters now

The strongest signal this weekend is not a flashy consumer-facing launch. It is that major retailers and footwear brands are putting more money and leadership attention into the operating stack underneath digital growth: marketplace fulfillment economics at Walmart, AI-enabled supply-chain and UX orchestration at Target, and core systems modernization at Crocs. For a footwear leader, that means the next advantage is less about driving incremental traffic and more about becoming easier for powerful retail systems and AI surfaces to understand, fulfill and monetize.

Executive takeaway: prioritize operating-control investments that compound across channels — catalog quality, inventory confidence, fulfillment eligibility, first-party identity and modernized commercial systems — because those are increasingly the inputs that determine discovery, conversion and margin.

Walmart’s latest quarter says ecosystem economics are still strengthening even as demand looks more fragile

On August 20, Walmart said Q2 FY27 global eCommerce grew 23%, Walmart U.S. eCommerce grew 24%, store-fulfilled delivery increased 40%, marketplace net sales grew more than 50%, and nearly 50% of marketplace volume flowed through fulfillment services.

Walmart Q2 FY27 earnings release

In the same release, Walmart said global advertising grew 38%, Walmart Connect U.S. grew 43% excluding VIZIO, and global membership fee revenue grew 17%.

Walmart Q2 FY27 earnings release

Reuters reported on August 20 that Walmart’s U.S. comp sales growth of 2.6% missed analyst expectations, marking its first same-store sales miss in at least five years, while management pointed to higher fuel costs and trade-offs by consumers.

Reuters on Walmart’s Q2 demand backdrop

The read-through is important: the operating model is still compounding even while the consumer backdrop gets shakier. Marketplace, fulfillment, advertising and membership are making Walmart stronger, but the softer comp result is a warning that brands cannot rely on broad category tailwinds. They need sharper assortment, price architecture and in-stock execution inside the platforms that are still winning trips.

For footwear teams, separate the channel question into two parts: where demand is softening, and where ecosystem economics are still improving. The second group deserves disproportionate attention because those partners can keep taking share even in a slower demand environment.

Target is wiring AI and supply-chain simulation into the retail control tower

Target said on August 13 that its internally built Proxima digital twin improved on-shelf availability by 2.5% in a pilot across 63 fresh items and modeled inventory flow for the Houston Receive Center with about 98% accuracy before launch.

Target on Proxima digital twin technology

Target also said Proxima could inform future AI-powered, agentic decision-support capabilities for inventory and operational decisions.

Target on Proxima digital twin technology

On August 11, Target announced Chandhu Nair as its first chief AI officer, effective August 24, and elevated Purvi Shah to senior vice president of UX to strengthen AI and user-experience capabilities across the enterprise.

Target AI and UX leadership announcement

This is more than a tech story. Target is explicitly joining the inventory engine to the customer interface. That matters because the retailer that can simulate availability, coordinate AI decisioning and shape the UX layer will have more influence over what gets surfaced, promised and sold.

Pressure-test whether your product content, inventory feeds and service-level promises are robust enough to survive inside retailer-controlled AI and replenishment systems. If they are not, brand equity alone will not protect conversion.

Crocs’ long-term systems bet is a useful footwear signal: modernize the backbone before the next growth turn

On August 6, Infosys said it entered a 10-year strategic agreement with Crocs to modernize Crocs’ core business and IT systems, unify data, simplify operations, reduce costs and improve real-time decision-making across the organization.

Infosys on its 10-year Crocs agreement

Infosys said the program is intended to break down operational silos and create a more resilient digital backbone as Crocs scales globally.

Infosys on its 10-year Crocs agreement

The strategic point is not the vendor. It is that a footwear winner is willing to commit to a decade-long operating reset while the market is still debating top-line demand. That is usually what brands do when they believe future growth will be decided by speed, data coherence and execution consistency across wholesale, DTC and marketplaces.

If your digital roadmap is still organized around isolated front-end features, it is probably too shallow. The harder but better move is to modernize the data and process spine that powers merchandising, supply chain, customer service and marketplace operations together.

Evidence notes

Walmart and Target items are grounded in primary company disclosures, with Reuters used only to contextualize Walmart’s weaker-than-expected comp result. The Crocs item comes from an Infosys press release rather than a Crocs filing or Crocs-issued release, so the existence and stated goals of the partnership are attributable, but near-term business impact remains unproven and should be treated as directional rather than confirmed performance evidence.
