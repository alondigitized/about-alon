Retail Intelligence Pulse

Saturday, August 08 2026 · Footwear, digital commerce, marketplaces

What newly matters now

The late-July footwear readout is clearer now than it looked in real time. adidas just posted record second-quarter sales with 25% DTC growth and only 6% wholesale growth because it chose to protect full-price business instead of flooding the market. At the same time, Nordstrom’s Anniversary Sale is ending August 9 after another loyalty-gated markdown cycle that includes Nike, adidas, UGG and Puma. Put bluntly: premium footwear demand is still there, but price architecture is fragile. Brands that keep product heat and channel discipline can win; brands that let promotional retail set the tone will train consumers to wait.

Executive takeaway: treat full-price sell-through, loyalty-gated partner promotions and marketplace/storefront control as one pricing system, not three separate teams.

adidas just delivered the strongest current proof that full-price premium demand still exists

adidas said on July 30 that second-quarter 2026 currency-neutral revenue rose 14%, producing record quarterly net sales of €6.743 billion.

The company said DTC revenue grew 25%, including e-commerce up 27% and own retail up 23%, while wholesale revenue increased 6% because adidas kept a conservative sell-in approach in a promotional marketplace.

Performance revenue grew 39%, led by Football and Running, and gross margin improved 0.8 percentage points to 52.5% even as adidas spent €212 million more on marketing tied largely to World Cup campaigns and activations.

adidas Q2 2026 results

This is the cleanest current evidence that premium athletic demand has not collapsed; it has become more selective. adidas is showing that when the product engine is hot enough, a brand can hold back wholesale, keep DTC moving faster and still expand margin. That matters for every footwear operator arguing that discounting is the only way to keep volume alive.

Benchmark your business against this exact pattern: Is DTC outgrowing wholesale because consumers want the brand more, or because you are subsidizing the shift with promotions and marketing spend that never pays back?

adidas paired that quarter with a CFO move that points toward tighter retail discipline, not looser growth chasing

adidas said on July 30 that Birgit Kretschmer will join the Executive Board effective September 1, 2026 and succeed Harm Ohlmeyer as CFO at the end of the year.

Kretschmer spent 25 years at adidas in finance leadership roles and has spent the last six years as CFO of C&A, which adidas CEO Bjørn Gulden said added "cost discipline working as the CFO of a vertical retailer."

The same release noted Ohlmeyer’s role in building adidas’ e-commerce business roughly 15 years ago and overseeing divestitures that sharpened focus on the adidas brand.

adidas CFO succession release

This is more than routine succession. Coming right alongside a quarter defined by disciplined sell-in, higher DTC volume and heavy brand investment, the choice reads like an operating-model signal: adidas wants someone who understands both brand finance and vertical-retail discipline. In this environment, that usually means more focus on clean inventories, sharper channel economics and fewer sloppy growth tradeoffs.

Watch competitors’ finance and commercial leadership moves more closely. In this cycle, org design is strategy: the executives being elevated tell you which tradeoffs the company intends to make next.

Nordstrom’s sale calendar is a live reminder that pricing power is still contested at retail

Nordstrom said its 2026 Anniversary Sale runs from July 18 through August 9, with cardmember Early Access beginning as early as July 14 based on Nordy Club status.

Nordstrom described the event as its largest assortment to date, with over 100 brands and more than 50 new brands joining, and specifically highlighted footwear- and active-relevant brands including Puma, adidas, Nike, UGG and Veja.

Nordstrom said the sale is built to give its most loyal customers first access to discounts on new-season arrivals rather than end-of-season leftovers.

Nordstrom Anniversary Sale 2026 release

For footwear brands, this is not just a promotional event. It is a real-time market signal about how premium department-store retail now uses loyalty to stage markdowns earlier and more surgically, without fully trashing brand positioning. That can be useful distribution, but it also trains customers to anchor on member-only deal windows. If your brand is simultaneously trying to defend full-price DTC, these calendars matter.

Map the exact overlap between your owned-channel launch calendar and your partners’ loyalty-gated sale windows. If premium product is showing up in those windows too early or too often, you are quietly teaching consumers where the real price lives.

Evidence note: Nordstrom’s release is retailer-provided promotional material. It is strong evidence for sale structure, timing and featured brand presence, but not for item-level sell-through or margin outcomes.
