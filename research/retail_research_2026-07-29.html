Retail Intelligence Pulse

Wednesday, July 29 2026 · Footwear, digital commerce, marketplaces

What newly matters now

Nike, On and a Target-owned Shipt partnership all moved the same control levers this month, but from different starting points. Nike is explicitly rebuilding wholesale and cutting digital discounting to restore marketplace health. On is splitting commercial leadership so DTC, digital, retail and loyalty get clearer executive ownership. Shipt is pushing same-day footwear deeper into routine shopping through DSW. Put together, the pattern is clear: footwear players are no longer treating digital commerce as a standalone channel. They are redesigning the operating model around cleaner assortment control, faster fulfillment and tighter end-to-end ownership of the consumer journey.

Executive takeaway: if digital, marketplaces, loyalty and fulfillment still sit in separate strategy buckets, the org is already lagging the market.

Nike is still shrinking digital, but the more important signal is how deliberately it is rebuilding the marketplace

Nike reported on June 30 that fourth-quarter fiscal 2026 wholesale revenue rose 4% to $6.6 billion while NIKE Direct revenue fell 7% to $4.1 billion, including a 12% decline in NIKE Brand Digital.

On its official earnings call, CEO Elliott Hill said Nike refreshed more than 15,000 spaces in wholesale doors globally and elevated more than 150 stores with sport-led experiences in fiscal 2026.

Hill also said Nike is discounting less on NIKE Digital, plans to close doors no longer aligned to strategy, and intends to elevate 50% of the NIKE Direct owned fleet by the end of fiscal 2027.

Nike Q4 FY2026 results · Nike Q4 FY2026 transcript

This is not a simple DTC pullback. Nike is rebalancing the full marketplace: less promotional digital volume, more curated wholesale presentation, and a smaller but more premium owned-store fleet. For competitors, that raises the bar on how tightly channel mix and brand presentation have to be managed together.

Watch for whether your digital metrics reward low-quality volume. If traffic and conversion are being bought with discounting that damages marketplace health, Nike's reset is a warning, not an outlier.

On is separating global markets from customer ownership, which is exactly what a scaling premium challenger should do

On announced on July 7 that it is splitting its global commercial leadership into two distinct roles to align with its long-term consumer-centric ambitions.

Rebecca Cai was promoted to Chief Global Markets Officer, while Alice Delahunt will join as Chief Customer Officer overseeing direct-to-consumer channels across digital and retail plus customer loyalty.

On said the new structure is intended to enable greater focus and alignment, and that the leaders will work with product, marketing and supply chain teams to simplify the operating model.

On leadership update

This is a meaningful org-design tell. As On scales, it is refusing to leave DTC, digital, retail and loyalty buried inside a generic commercial structure. Premium growth now requires separate executive focus on the customer system, not just regional selling.

Pressure-test whether your growth org has a real owner for the full customer loop. If retail, digital trading, CRM and loyalty are still spread across too many leaders, speed and accountability are leaking.

Shipt's DSW move shows footwear is getting pulled into the same-day convenience economy

Shipt announced on July 28 a new partnership with DSW that adds same-day delivery of footwear and accessories through the Shipt app and Shipt.com.

The company said nearly 75 million households nationwide will be able to access same-day delivery from DSW stores, and that the assortment includes brands such as New Balance, Crocs and Adidas.

Shipt described itself as a marketplace of more than 100 retailers and noted it is an independently operated subsidiary of Target.

Shipt and DSW partnership announcement

Footwear is being normalized as a same-day, replenishment-like category for urgent family moments, not just a planned browse purchase. That matters because convenience expectations can shift category economics before many brands notice.

Treat same-day eligibility as a strategic decision, not a last-mile add-on. If priority styles are absent from fast-delivery ecosystems during key seasonal peaks, competitors can capture the demand before brand preference even gets a chance to matter.
