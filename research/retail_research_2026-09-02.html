Retail Intelligence Pulse

Wednesday, September 02 2026 · Footwear, digital commerce, marketplaces

What newly matters now

The cleanest shift this morning is structural, not promotional. U.S. e-commerce growth has re-accelerated, large retailers are proving that marketplace-plus-membership-plus-fulfillment can compound faster than core merchandise, and Nike just put wholesale and direct back under one commercial owner. At the same time, the FTC’s new Amazon ad-auction suit says the retail-media layer is now big enough to become a governance issue, not just a margin story.

Executive takeaway: stop treating digital commerce, wholesale, loyalty, retail media and fulfillment as separate scorecards. The market is rewarding integrated commercial systems and starting to punish opaque monetization inside them.

U.S. e-commerce growth has clearly sped up again

The U.S. Census Bureau said on August 18 that second-quarter 2026 retail e-commerce sales reached $340.2 billion, up 3.8% sequentially and 12.2% year over year, versus 6.7% growth for total retail sales. E-commerce accounted for 17.1% of total retail sales in the quarter.

U.S. Census Bureau: Quarterly Retail E-Commerce Sales, Q2 2026 (August 18, 2026)

That does not mean every retailer is winning online. It means the channel is taking share again fast enough that the operating model matters more. The winners are the players that combine assortment breadth, convenience, identity and monetization into one system.

Target said on August 19 that digital comparable sales grew 8.7% in Q2, led by more than 25% growth in same-day delivery, while non-merchandise sales grew over 20% on strength in Roundel advertising, Target Circle 360 membership revenue and the Target+ marketplace. Walmart said on August 20 that marketplace, fulfillment services, membership, advertising and other commerce solutions are strengthening company economics, and that nearly 50% of marketplace volume flowed through fulfillment services in Q2 FY27.

Target Q2 2026 earnings release (August 19, 2026)
Walmart Q2 FY27 earnings release (August 20, 2026)

For footwear, this raises the cost of weak channel plumbing. If search, inventory visibility, same-day options, membership and marketplace content are not coordinated, the category will lose demand to ecosystems that are.

Nike has explicitly re-unified wholesale and direct under one commercial operator

Nike said on August 27 that Jane Ewing will join as Executive Vice President and Chief Commercial Officer effective September 7. Nike said Ewing will lead its global Marketplace organization, including Sales and Nike Direct, after holding senior leadership roles at Walmart spanning merchandising, digital acceleration and international operations.

Nike senior leadership announcement (August 27, 2026)

That is the strategic signal. Nike is not talking about direct and wholesale as separate empires anymore. It is formalizing one marketplace owner to reconnect partner relationships, digital experience and physical retail execution.

This is the org question worth asking now: who in your business actually owns the total marketplace across DTC, wholesale, digital shelf, pricing and inventory flow? If the answer is “several people,” speed is probably leaking.

Amazon’s latest ad-auction case turns retail media into a board-level trust issue

On August 31, the FTC and 22 states sued Amazon, alleging it secretly inflated prices in online search advertising auctions for more than one million brands and sellers over multiple years. The FTC said the complaint alleges Amazon charged Sponsored Products advertisers their own winning bid close to 80% of the time and that the scheme likely extracted tens of billions of dollars. Amazon publicly disputed the case on September 1, calling it misguided and saying average Sponsored Products cost per click was flat from 2019 through 2024, adjusted for inflation, while conversion rates improved.

FTC press release on Amazon ad-auction lawsuit (August 31, 2026)
Amazon response to FTC Sponsored Ads lawsuit (September 1, 2026)

Whatever the legal outcome, the strategic point is already clear: retail media is no longer a side business. It is now material enough that auction design, pricing transparency and advertiser trust can become enterprise risk.

Any brand or retailer building commerce-media economics should audit pricing logic, measurement claims and advertiser communications before regulators or partners do it for them.
