Retail Intelligence Pulse

Tuesday, August 11 2026 · Footwear, digital commerce, marketplaces

What newly matters now

Today’s cleanest new read comes from On: premium performance footwear is still growing fast without sacrificing full-price integrity, and the owned channel is doing more of the work. At the same time, Amazon and Target are trying to own the consumer interface around that demand through AI-assisted discovery, loyalty-linked shopping flows and faster fulfillment. The strategic point is simple: product heat is not enough anymore. The winner also controls where discovery happens and how frictionlessly the basket closes.

Executive takeaway: treat premium footwear momentum and AI-mediated retail discovery as one system. Protect margin and brand heat like On, but assume more demand will be captured inside retailer-owned AI, marketplace and fulfillment surfaces rather than only on brand sites.

On just delivered the strongest proof point in footwear this morning: premium growth plus tighter DTC economics

On reported on August 11, 2026 that second-quarter net sales increased 13.5% year over year to CHF 850.3 million, or 21.6% on a constant-currency basis.

Direct-to-consumer sales rose 26.0% to CHF 388.4 million, while wholesale increased 4.8% to CHF 461.9 million. DTC reached 45.7% of net sales, a new second-quarter high for the company.

Gross margin expanded to 65.4% from 61.5%, despite higher U.S. import tariffs and excluding any tariff refunds. On raised its full-year gross margin expectation to at least 65.0% while continuing to expect low-20% constant-currency sales growth.

On Q2 2026 results

That is not normal footwear performance in this environment. On is showing that a brand can still grow globally, hold price, absorb tariff pressure and expand margin if product innovation, brand heat and owned-channel productivity are all working together. More importantly, management explicitly said it is deliberately managing wholesale sell-in to protect a clean runway for future innovation. That is a discipline signal, not just a growth signal.

Benchmark against On on three things, not one: DTC mix, full-price integrity and deliberate wholesale management. If your business is growing only when wholesale broadens or markdowns deepen, you are not playing the same quality game.

Amazon is scaling the convenience layer so aggressively that discovery and fulfillment are starting to merge

Amazon said in its second-quarter 2026 results that it delivered over 40% more items same-day or overnight in the first six months of the year than the same period last year.

Andy Jassy also said Amazon expanded ultra-low-priced selection on Amazon Haul in the U.S. by nearly 20x since launch to more than six million items priced under $10, and that Amazon Now added 80 new U.S. cities and towns in Q2.

Amazon’s shopping assistant, now called Alexa for Shopping, can help customers compare products, set price-triggered purchases and in some cases either buy on their behalf or send them directly to a merchant site.

Amazon is turning the old sequence of search, compare, click and wait into one integrated operating surface. That matters for footwear even if the sale closes elsewhere, because the platform that answers the query, recommends the product and compresses delivery expectations starts shaping which brands get seen and trusted. The risk is not just price competition. The risk is becoming invisible in a machine-mediated shopping path.

Recheck how your products show up in AI-assisted search, off-Amazon brand comparisons and fast-delivery promise zones. If the content, assortment or availability is weak, the convenience layer will route demand around you.

Target is building a more curated version of the same control stack: AI discovery tied to loyalty and marketplace curation

Target said AI-driven traffic to its sites jumped 2,000% in the first quarter versus last year, compared with nearly 400% growth in AI-driven traffic to retail sites overall.

The company said it is the first mass retailer with shopping experiences across Google Search and Gemini, Microsoft Copilot and OpenAI’s ChatGPT, including browsing, basket-building and linking to Target Circle loyalty accounts.

Target also said on July 1 that Target Plus is being positioned to show up more clearly in AI-powered search and other digital discovery experiences, while Q1 non-merchandise sales grew nearly 25% on strong growth in Roundel, Target Circle 360 membership revenue and the Target+ marketplace.

Target is not trying to beat Amazon on infinite assortment. It is trying to make curated assortment, loyalty identity and AI-native discovery reinforce each other. For brands, this means the digital shelf is becoming more selective: it is not enough to be listed; you need to be surfaced inside the retailer’s preferred discovery architecture.

For key retail partners, audit whether your assortment is machine-legible and loyalty-relevant: clear product data, strong imagery, differentiated value story, and inventory that supports fast fulfillment. Curated marketplaces will reward brands that help the retailer look smart inside AI, not just brands with broad catalogs.

Evidence note: Target’s AI traffic and marketplace commentary come from company communications, not audited third-party measurement. They are still high-signal because they describe active platform strategy and real deployment choices, but some adoption and conversion claims should be treated as directional until broader transaction data emerges.
