Retail Intelligence Pulse

Sunday, July 26 2026 · Footwear, digital commerce, marketplaces

What newly matters now

This week produced three clean operating signals. Nike is shrinking online distribution in China back into official channels because management says the marketplace became too fragmented to sustain a premium brand experience. Puma has created a top-level e-commerce role and explicitly separated global e-commerce from retail inside DTC. Target, meanwhile, added a board director whose stated value is omnichannel, loyalty, operations and fresh-food execution as Michael Fiddelke pushes the next phase of turnaround. The common thread is not "AI" in the abstract. It is control: who owns digital discovery, who owns the transaction, and who has the authority to protect margin and trust when channels get messy.

Immediate takeaway: treat digital commerce leadership, marketplace governance and loyalty-linked operating discipline as one agenda, not three separate workstreams.

Nike is choosing channel control over online breadth in China

Reuters reported on July 21 that starting in January, key sportswear retailers in China will stop selling Nike products online, while Nike-branded storefronts on Tmall, JD.com and Douyin, plus Nike's own website and app, will handle online sales.

Cathy Sparks, Nike's vice president and general manager of Greater China, told Reuters the marketplace had become "fragmented and cluttered," and said consumers want a premium, trustworthy experience that is connected across digital and physical channels.

Reuters also reported that Greater China fourth-quarter sales fell 17% on a constant-currency basis, worse than the prior quarter's 10% decline, and that the majority of Nike's 16 store partners in China will stop selling online.

Reuters via Euronext on Nike China online distribution reset

This is a much bigger signal than a normal channel cleanup. Nike is effectively saying that unmanaged marketplace sprawl can damage price integrity and brand trust enough that it is worth sacrificing online breadth to regain control. For footwear leaders, that raises the bar on channel architecture in marketplaces where unauthorized discounting, inconsistent presentation and duplicated storefronts have been tolerated as the cost of reach.

Audit where your brand is still accepting fragmented digital distribution in the name of volume. The strategic question is not just GMV. It is whether the digital shelf still reinforces trust, premium positioning and full-price sell-through.

Puma is formalizing e-commerce as a standalone operating capability

Puma announced on July 22 that it appointed Dusan Hamlin to the newly created role of Vice President E-Commerce, effective immediately.

Puma said the appointment follows its late-2025 decision to split direct-to-consumer into two dedicated areas: Global Retail and Global E-Commerce.

The company said dedicated e-commerce leadership is meant to further develop strategy and improve consumer experience across Puma's website, apps and marketplaces, with social commerce and AI-driven commerce at scale identified as focus areas.

Puma e-commerce leadership announcement

Puma is making an org-design statement that many brands still avoid: stores and digital are both part of DTC, but they are no longer the same management problem. Websites, apps, marketplaces, performance marketing and AI-mediated shopping require channel-specific leadership and platform accountability. That matters because a lot of footwear organizations still talk omnichannel while structurally burying e-commerce inside broader retail leadership.

Pressure-test whether e-commerce in your model has real decision rights over trading, discovery, marketplaces and platform roadmap, or whether it is still a service layer under a store-led commercial structure.

Target is adding omnichannel and loyalty operating experience at the board level

Target said on July 22 that former 7-Eleven CEO Joe DePinto will join its board on August 1 and serve on the Infrastructure & Finance and Audit & Risk committees.

Target said DePinto adds expertise in operations, loyalty, fresh food and omnichannel capabilities as the company pursues its next growth phase under CEO Michael Fiddelke.

Target said DePinto led 7-Eleven through expansion, digital innovation, loyalty investments and omnichannel capability building.

Target board appointment announcement

Board composition is easy to ignore, but this one is strategically revealing. Target is signaling that its turnaround is not just a merchandising exercise. It sees operations, loyalty and omnichannel execution as governance-level priorities. That should matter to footwear brands selling through major retail partners, because the partner set that wins from here will be the one that can combine value, convenience, food-frequency traffic and digitally mediated loyalty into one system.

Watch large retail partners not just for sales results, but for who they are putting into decision-making seats. Leadership inputs often tell you where operating capital and tech attention are about to go.
