Retail Intelligence Pulse

Friday, July 31 2026 · Footwear, digital commerce, marketplaces

What newly matters now

Three fresh signals landed in the same ten-day window and they fit together unusually cleanly. Amazon's July 30 quarter showed how much retail power now comes from compressing discovery, delivery and seller infrastructure into one machine. PUMA's July 31 half-year update showed the harder brand-side version of the same story: pulling back undesirable wholesale, protecting DTC mix and using marketplaces more selectively can hurt sales near term but improve business quality. Deckers' latest quarter remains the strongest counterexample to panic: it is still growing both DTC and wholesale while keeping inventory tighter than last year. The common lesson is that digital commerce is no longer mainly a traffic problem. It is an operating-control problem.

Executive takeaway: treat fulfillment speed, channel hygiene, marketplace governance and DTC economics as one agenda. If they sit in separate scorecards, you are managing the symptoms instead of the system.

Amazon just widened the moat between traffic capture and retail execution

Amazon said on July 30 that second-quarter 2026 net sales rose 20% year over year to $200.6 billion, with North America sales up 16% to $116.2 billion and operating income up 43% to $27.5 billion.

In the same release, Amazon said Prime members received over 40% more items via same-day or overnight delivery in the first half of 2026, and that Amazon Now expanded by 80 new U.S. cities and towns during the quarter.

Amazon also said it combined Rufus and Alexa+ into "Alexa for Shopping," where active users nearly doubled and interactions rose more than 5x year over year, while launching Amazon Supply Chain Services so outside businesses can move, store and deliver goods on Amazon's network.

Amazon Q2 2026 results

This is the clearest current evidence that Amazon is not treating AI shopping, fast fulfillment and merchant services as separate bets. It is welding them into a single control surface. For footwear brands, that means the platform risk is no longer just margin pressure inside a marketplace. It is the possibility that discovery, basket formation, delivery promise and post-purchase convenience all get bundled into one default customer habit.

Pressure-test where your brands still rely on slower handoffs between media, commerce and fulfillment. If Amazon can collapse those steps and you cannot, your conversion gap will widen even if your brand heat stays intact.

PUMA's reset is sacrificing topline to improve channel quality and DTC mix

PUMA reported on July 31 that Q2 2026 currency-adjusted sales fell 9.4% to €1.69 billion, while wholesale declined 14.0% currency-adjusted to €1.09 billion.

PUMA said DTC sales rose 0.4% currency-adjusted to €595.8 million, with e-commerce up 1.8% currency-adjusted despite lower promotions, and that DTC mix increased to 35.2% of sales from 32.1% a year earlier.

The company explicitly said it continued reducing undesirable wholesale business, especially in North America and Europe, while noting e-commerce was supported by additional marketplaces in APAC and growth in Greater China. Inventories fell 15.3% and free cash flow rose to €328.8 million from €94.9 million a year earlier.

PUMA Q2 2026 release

This is not a clean growth story, but it is a strategically honest one. PUMA is showing what happens when a brand stops pretending all revenue is equally valuable. Fewer bad wholesale doors, better inventory control and selective marketplace growth can improve cash and mix before they improve optics.

If your wholesale cleanup logic exists only in PowerPoint, PUMA's quarter is the real-world benchmark. Map which accounts, marketplaces and promo behaviors are genuinely additive versus simply inflating volume while degrading pricing and presentation.

Deckers still looks like the healthier version of the same playbook

Deckers said on July 23 that first-quarter fiscal 2027 net sales increased 5.7% to $1.02 billion, with HOKA up 7.7% to $703.5 million and UGG up 4.9% to $278.0 million.

Deckers also said DTC net sales increased 13.0% to $352.8 million, wholesale net sales increased 2.2% to $666.7 million, DTC comparable net sales rose 6.8%, and inventories declined to $807.6 million from $849.4 million a year earlier.

The company raised full-year diluted EPS guidance to $7.35-$7.50 and said HOKA is still expected to grow by a low-double-digit percentage for the full year.

Deckers Q1 FY2027 results

Deckers matters because it shows the more favorable end-state that PUMA is trying to rebuild toward: a business where DTC is growing fast, wholesale is still additive, inventory is controlled and premium brands do not need sloppy distribution to keep momentum.

Use Deckers as the benchmark for channel balance. The goal is not maximum DTC at any cost; it is a portfolio where DTC sharpens economics and data ownership while wholesale still expands reach without corrupting the brand.

Evidence notes

Amazon, PUMA and Deckers items are based on official company earnings materials. The strategic interpretation is mine, inferred from the reported facts rather than stated by the companies. Deckers' release is from July 23, 2026, so it is not a same-day development; it is included because it remains one of the clearest current footwear benchmarks for healthy DTC-plus-wholesale execution.
