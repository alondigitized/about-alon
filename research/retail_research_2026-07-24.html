Retail Intelligence Pulse

Friday, July 24 2026 · Footwear, digital commerce, marketplaces

What newly matters now

Two fresh signals matter more than the volume of retail AI chatter. First, Deckers just posted another quarter where direct-to-consumer outgrew wholesale and gave management enough confidence to lift profit guidance again. That reinforces that the current footwear share winners are pairing product heat with healthier owned-channel economics. Second, Nordstrom has now officially launched its digital marketplace, but with an important twist: it is preserving loyalty benefits, customer care, and service parity rather than treating marketplace inventory as a second-class experience. For footwear and apparel leaders, the issue is not whether to expand assortment or channels. It is whether you can do it without breaking brand control, loyalty value, or margin discipline.

Immediate takeaway: keep a hard line on where DTC should win structurally, and if marketplace breadth is part of the strategy, make sure service, returns, and loyalty stay unified instead of fragmenting the customer journey.

Deckers keeps proving that strong footwear brands can still grow DTC and protect margins

On July 23, Deckers reported first-quarter fiscal 2027 net sales of $1.02 billion, up from $964.5 million a year earlier.

Wholesale net sales increased 2.2% to $666.7 million, while direct-to-consumer net sales increased 13.0% to $352.8 million. DTC comparable net sales increased 6.8%.

Deckers said inventories were $807.6 million at June 30, down from $849.4 million a year earlier.

The company raised its full-year diluted EPS outlook to $7.35 to $7.50 from $7.30 to $7.45, while saying gross margin is now expected to be slightly better than 56.5% and operating margin slightly better than 21.5%.

Reuters reported that demand for HOKA and UGG plus growth in the higher-margin DTC business helped profitability, and that Deckers has continued to gain share while Nike deals with softer demand for legacy sneaker lines.

This is bigger than a good quarter. Deckers is showing that in performance and premium footwear, brands can still drive growth through both wholesale and owned channels without giving up profitability. The inventory decline matters too: this is not growth being bought with balance-sheet sloppiness.

Treat Deckers as a live benchmark for what “healthy channel mix” now looks like: differentiated product, disciplined inventory, and a DTC engine strong enough to lift margins rather than just absorb marketing spend.

Nordstrom is expanding assortment through marketplace without surrendering loyalty and service

Nordstrom said it has officially launched a digital marketplace on Nordstrom.com and its app, using an unowned inventory model to expand assortment beyond traditional wholesale partnerships.

The company said customers shopping marketplace items will have the same access to loyalty benefits, customer care support, fast and easy returns, styling, alterations, and Nordstrom Notes as they do on any other Nordstrom purchase.

Nordstrom also said the marketplace will be paired with personalized recommendations, improved search, and broader digital curation to make the larger assortment easier to navigate.

Nordstrom digital marketplace launch

A lot of marketplaces dilute the core retail proposition. Nordstrom is trying the opposite move: use marketplace economics to widen selection while preserving the same loyalty and service wrapper customers already trust. That is strategically important because it suggests premium retailers still believe the winning model is curated breadth plus unified experience, not endless catalog sprawl.

If marketplace expansion is on the table, copy the principle rather than the label: customers should not have to decode which benefits, returns paths, or service levels apply based on inventory ownership.

Source quality is strongest on Deckers because the core facts come from the company’s earnings release and are reinforced by Reuters reporting. Nordstrom’s marketplace details come directly from the company, so the feature set is credible, but the strategic success of the model is still interpretation rather than proof. The comparison to Nike and the broader conclusion about premium marketplace design are analysis from these facts, not company admissions.
