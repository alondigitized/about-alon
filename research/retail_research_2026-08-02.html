Retail Intelligence Pulse

Sunday, August 02 2026 · Footwear, digital commerce, marketplaces

What newly matters now

Three late-week signals now fit together more clearly. Amazon's second-quarter results show that AI shopping, Prime economics, advertising and faster delivery are becoming one commercial system, not separate capabilities. Crocs just posted another quarter where direct-to-consumer outgrew wholesale while guidance moved up. PUMA, by contrast, is still taking near-term pain to clean up low-quality wholesale exposure and lift DTC mix. The combined message for footwear leaders is blunt: digital advantage is now less about adding channels and more about controlling where discovery happens, which doors deserve inventory, and how quickly the supply chain can fulfill profitable demand.

Executive takeaway: manage AI discovery, wholesale quality, DTC productivity and fulfillment speed as one operating model.

Amazon is turning AI shopping, Prime and fast fulfillment into one flywheel

Amazon said on July 30 that second-quarter 2026 net sales increased 20% year over year to $200.6 billion, with North America sales up 16% to $116.2 billion.

Amazon said it set record delivery speeds for Prime members in the first half of 2026, with more than 40% more items delivered same-day or overnight, while Grocery and Everyday Essentials grew meaningfully faster than the rest of the business.

Amazon said it combined Rufus and Alexa+ into Alexa for Shopping, and that active users were close to doubling while interactions increased more than 5x year over year in the second quarter.

Amazon also said U.S. customers who use Alexa for Shopping spend an average of more than 40% more per order than those who do not, and customers who tried Alexa+ are signing up for Prime at a nearly 25% higher rate.

CEO Andy Jassy said advertising grew 26% year over year in the quarter.

Amazon Q2 2026 earnings release

This matters because Amazon is no longer just improving search or shipping one feature at a time. It is fusing AI discovery, loyalty conversion, basket formation, media monetization and faster fulfillment into a single closed loop. For brands, that raises the bar on content quality, availability, price credibility and marketplace execution because the recommendation engine and the transaction engine are now converging.

Pressure-test whether your product data, inventory positions, paid media and marketplace assortment are good enough to win inside machine-mediated shopping journeys rather than only in traditional search and browse.

Crocs is still the strongest current proof that channel discipline can produce growth without margin sacrifice

Crocs said on July 30 that second-quarter 2026 consolidated revenue increased 2.6% to $1.179 billion, while direct-to-consumer revenue grew 12.0% and wholesale revenue declined 7.2%.

The Crocs brand surpassed $1 billion in quarterly revenue for the first time, with brand direct-to-consumer revenue up 12.9% to $559 million.

Crocs also said inventories declined to $389 million from $405 million a year earlier and raised full-year 2026 guidance to revenue growth of approximately 1% to 2%, up from prior guidance of down 1% to up 1%.

Crocs Q2 2026 results

Crocs keeps showing that a healthy footwear business does not need to chase every wholesale dollar. If brand demand is real and inventory stays controlled, DTC can grow faster, guidance can move higher and wholesale can stay selective instead of acting as the default growth valve.

Use Crocs as the benchmark case for brand-right channel management: better DTC output, cleaner inventory and enough confidence to raise the year rather than defend it.

PUMA is showing what channel cleanup looks like before the payoff is visible in the top line

PUMA said on July 31 that second-quarter 2026 currency-adjusted sales fell 9.4% to €1.69 billion and wholesale sales declined 14.0% currency-adjusted to €1.09 billion.

At the same time, PUMA said direct-to-consumer sales increased 0.4% currency-adjusted to €595.8 million, e-commerce grew 1.8% despite lower promotions, and DTC mix rose to 35.2% from 32.1% a year earlier.

PUMA also said inventories declined 15.3% to €1.82 billion, free cash flow rose to €328.8 million from €94.9 million, and it continued reducing undesirable wholesale business, especially in North America and Europe.

PUMA Q2 2026 results

PUMA is the uncomfortable mirror image of Crocs. The quarter is weaker, but strategically it shows that cleaning up bad doors, reducing promotions and lifting DTC mix can improve business quality before they improve reported growth. That is relevant for any footwear brand still overexposed to partners or digital channels that damage pricing, presentation or consumer data ownership.

Treat weak but cleaner volume as strategically better than strong but low-quality volume. If channel repair is needed, do it early, before excess inventory and marketplace sprawl dictate the timetable.

Evidence notes

All hard facts above come from official company earnings materials published on July 30-31, 2026. The strategic read is interpretive. Amazon does not break out how much of its retail growth came directly from Alexa for Shopping or Rufus, so the commercial importance of those tools is inferred from the engagement, spend and Prime-conversion metrics it disclosed.
