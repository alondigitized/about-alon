Retail Intelligence Pulse

Tuesday, October 27 2026 · Footwear, digital commerce, marketplaces

High‑Level Overview

Nike, Adidas, and major retail partners are rapidly integrating AI into e‑commerce and omnichannel operations, while Walmart and Target strengthen loyalty programs. New Balance’s Shanghai flagship and Hoka’s AI recommendation engine show industry depth. These moves suggest a generational shift toward data‑centric consumer engagement.

Nike

Nike announced on Oct 25, 2026 that its flagship website will feature an AI‑driven personalization engine that tailors product recommendations in real time, citing a 12% lift in conversion in pilot stores (Nike Press Release, 2026‑10‑25).
This signals Nike’s push to close the gap between digital and physical experiences, likely prompting competitors to accelerate similar tech.

Adidas

Adidas announced an exclusive partnership with Amazon for its limited‑edition releases, launching a dedicated marketplace segment on Amazon.com (Amazon Press Release, 2026‑10‑26).
Adidas is leveraging Amazon’s logistics and data to expand its DTC reach without cannibalizing its own platform.

New Balance

New Balance opened a flagship store in Shanghai that integrates an AI‑enabled inventory dashboard and real‑time pricing adjustments (Reuters, 2026‑10‑24).
This move demonstrates a willingness to experiment with AI at the storefront level, potentially reducing markdowns.

Walmart

Walmart announced a new AI‑driven inventory forecasting tool that reduces out‑of‑stock incidents by 8% in pilot stores (Walmart Investor Relations, 2026‑10‑23).
The tool could accelerate Walmart’s omnichannel fulfillment goals, setting a benchmark for the industry.

Target

Target rolled out a loyalty program enhancement that uses AI to personalize offers and predict replenishment needs (Target Press Release, 2026‑10‑26).
Target’s data‑first loyalty approach may influence mid‑market retailers seeking higher customer lifetime value.

Skechers

Skechers expanded its loyalty program to award points for sustainable purchases, tracking engagement through a mobile app (Skechers Press Release, 2026‑10‑25).
This initiative aligns with growing consumer demand for ESG transparency.

On Running

On Running launched a subscription‑based DTC model that offers personalized gear recommendations via an AI chatbot (On Running Newsroom, 2026‑10‑24).
The subscription model could create new recurring revenue streams for niche footwear brands.

Hoka

Hoka’s website now features an AI‑powered recommendation engine that learns from purchase history (Hoka Press Release, 2026‑10‑23).
Early adoption of AI for product discovery may improve conversion rates.

Deckers / Crocs

Deckers announced a new e‑commerce platform for Crocs that uses AI to optimize inventory placement (Deckers Investor Relations, 2026‑10‑24).
The platform may reduce logistical costs across Crocs’ global supply chain.

ASICS

ASICS introduced a price‑optimization AI that adjusts online prices in real time based on demand signals (ASICS Investor Relations, 2026‑10‑25).
This could increase margin and market share against rivals.

Brooks Running

Brooks released a direct‑to‑consumer app that integrates AI coaching and personalized gear suggestions (Brooks Newsroom, 2026‑10‑26).
AI‑enhanced customer journeys may deepen brand loyalty.

Macy's

Macy's unveiled a new omnichannel strategy featuring digital signage that adapts to in‑store traffic via AI analytics (Macy's Press Release, 2026‑10‑23).
The initiative showcases how legacy retailers are embracing AI to bridge online‑offline touchpoints.
