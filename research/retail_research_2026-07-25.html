Retail Intelligence Pulse

Saturday, July 25 2026 · Footwear, digital commerce, marketplaces

What newly matters now

Three fresh signals are worth attention. First, Deckers just showed again that premium performance footwear can still grow faster in direct-to-consumer than in wholesale while holding gross margin above 56%. Second, the U.S. tariff reset that took effect on Friday creates a new sourcing risk for brands with heavy Vietnam exposure, and Nike is one of the clearest examples. Third, Puma has now made ecommerce leadership explicit at the top of the org chart, separating it from retail and putting marketplaces, apps, and AI-driven commerce under a dedicated operator. Put together, the message is simple: footwear leaders now need stronger owned-channel economics, tighter sourcing resilience, and more specialized digital operating models at the same time.

Immediate takeaway: pressure-test channel mix, sourcing exposure, and digital org design together rather than as separate workstreams.

Deckers is still the cleanest proof that strong footwear demand plus DTC discipline can fund growth

Deckers reported on July 23 that first-quarter fiscal 2027 net sales increased 5.7% to $1.020 billion.

HOKA net sales increased 7.7% to $703.5 million and UGG net sales increased 4.9% to $278.0 million.

Wholesale net sales increased 2.2% to $666.7 million, while direct-to-consumer net sales increased 13.0% to $352.8 million. DTC comparable net sales increased 6.8%.

Gross margin improved to 56.4% from 55.8%, and inventories were $807.6 million versus $849.4 million a year earlier.

Deckers raised full-year diluted EPS guidance to $7.35 to $7.50 and said gross margin is now expected to be slightly better than 56.5%.

Deckers Q1 FY2027 earnings release

This is more than a good quarter. Deckers is showing that performance footwear brands do not have to choose between channel growth and margin quality when product heat is real and inventory stays controlled. The key detail is not just that DTC grew faster than wholesale. It is that this happened while inventories were lower and guidance still moved up.

Use Deckers as a live benchmark for what healthy footwear economics look like now: product-led demand, selective wholesale, and a DTC business that lifts profitability instead of just absorbing traffic costs.

New U.S. tariffs just made Vietnam concentration a more immediate strategic risk

Reuters reported on July 24 that new U.S. tariffs of 10% and 12.5% took effect on 60 trading partners, and that Vietnam faces a 12.5% rate while Bangladesh, Cambodia, Indonesia, and Malaysia face 10% duties.

Reuters also reported that Vietnam was excluded from a new textile mechanism that could reduce some tariff exposure for certain competing sourcing markets tied to U.S. cotton and textile purchases.

Nike said in its fiscal 2026 Form 10-K that factories in Vietnam manufactured approximately 52% of total NIKE Brand footwear and 34% of total NIKE Brand apparel in fiscal 2026.

The fact pattern is clear even if brand-level financial impact is not yet. If Vietnam remains on a higher tariff footing than several competing apparel and footwear sourcing markets, brands with concentrated exposure lose flexibility on margin, pricing, and allocation. Nike is the most visible case because its disclosed Vietnam mix is so high, but the broader implication extends across the footwear sector.

Re-run sourcing concentration scenarios now. The question is not just tariff cost pass-through. It is how quickly brands can rebalance production, protect margin architecture, and avoid forcing blunt price moves into already promotional categories.

The tariff facts come from Reuters' reporting on the Federal Register notice and are strong. The implication for Nike and the wider sector is analytical inference based on Nike's disclosed sourcing mix, not a company statement about expected impact.

Puma is treating ecommerce as its own operating discipline, not a subset of retail

Puma said on July 22 that it appointed Dusan Hamlin to the newly created role of Vice President E-Commerce, effective immediately.

The company said Hamlin will drive the global strategy and commercial direction of Puma's digital and ecommerce businesses and that the move follows Puma's late-2025 decision to split DTC into Global Retail and Global E-Commerce.

Puma also said dedicated ecommerce leadership is intended to improve strategy and the consumer experience across its website, apps, and marketplaces, with social commerce and AI-driven commerce identified as focus areas.

Puma ecommerce leadership announcement

This is a meaningful org signal. Puma is effectively saying digital growth now needs its own senior operator, separate from store-led retail execution. That matters because many brands still talk about omnichannel as if one team can manage stores, apps, marketplaces, social commerce, and AI-native shopping with equal depth. Puma's restructure suggests that assumption is breaking.

Audit whether ecommerce in your organization is actually being run as a channel with dedicated accountability for trading, product discovery, marketplaces, and AI-enabled shopping flows, or whether it is still buried inside broader commercial leadership.
