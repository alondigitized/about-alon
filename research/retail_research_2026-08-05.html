Retail Intelligence Pulse

Wednesday, August 05 2026 · Footwear, digital commerce, marketplaces

What newly matters now

Three fresh signals from late July now line up cleanly in early-August trading conditions. Amazon just showed that AI shopping, media and fulfillment speed are scaling together inside one engine. PUMA's second-quarter reset showed what happens when a footwear brand cleans up undesirable wholesale faster than demand fully recovers. Walmart and Target are both attacking back-to-school with explicit price, convenience and event logic rather than generic assortment breadth. The common lesson is simple: digital growth is not a front-end problem anymore. It is an operating-discipline problem.

Executive takeaway: treat AI discovery, channel mix, pricing and fulfillment as one control system. If those teams still work on separate scorecards, you are slower than the market now requires.

Amazon is turning AI shopping into a scaled retail performance loop

Amazon said on July 30 that second-quarter 2026 net sales increased 20% year over year to $200.6 billion and operating income rose 43% to $27.5 billion.

Amazon also said advertising revenue grew 26% to $19.8 billion in Q2.

Management said over 350 million customers used Alexa for Shopping in the last 12 months, with active users nearly doubling in Q2 and interactions up more than fivefold year over year.

Amazon said it delivered over 40% more items same-day or overnight in the first six months of the year versus the prior-year period and expanded Amazon Now into 80 additional U.S. cities and towns in Q2.

This is strategically important because Amazon is no longer proving AI shopping as a UX novelty. It is showing a closed loop where conversational discovery, sponsored placement, delivery speed and repeat convenience reinforce each other. That raises the cost of weak content, weak availability and weak marketplace economics for every footwear brand selling into the platform.

Re-rank marketplace priorities around machine readability and speed-to-conversion: PDP completeness, inventory confidence, paid-search efficiency and fast-ship eligibility now compound together.

PUMA's quarter shows reset discipline can improve business quality before it restores growth

PUMA said on July 31 that Q2 2026 currency-adjusted sales declined 9.4% to €1.69 billion, while wholesale fell 14.0% and DTC rose 0.4%.

The company said it continued reducing undesirable wholesale business, particularly in North America and Europe, versus a prior-year quarter that still included a high share of mass-merchant business.

PUMA said e-commerce grew 1.8% in the quarter despite lower promotions, supported by additional marketplaces in APAC and growth in Greater China.

Inventories fell 15.3% to €1.82 billion and free cash flow rose to €328.8 million from €94.9 million a year earlier.

PUMA Q2 2026 release

The hard read is that PUMA is accepting top-line pain to improve channel quality and working-capital health. For footwear operators, that matters more than the headline sales decline. It suggests that cleaning up low-quality distribution can stabilize margin structure and cash generation before it shows up in growth again.

Pressure-test whether any wholesale volume is still being defended for optics rather than economics. If so, model the near-term revenue pain against the longer-term gain in inventory health, brand positioning and digital sell-through.

Back-to-school competition is being fought as a convenience-and-value operating system

Walmart said on July 15 that it is offering its lowest prices since 2019 on the 14 most popular school supplies, with select items starting at $0.25, plus more than 1,300 additional rollbacks versus last year.

Walmart also highlighted classroom-list shopping in the app, classroom registries, curbside pickup, Express Delivery in as little as 30 minutes and Walmart+ student membership benefits.

Target said on July 22 that 95% of its school supply deals are at or below last year's retail prices and that guests could save up to 25% off kids' shoes during its July 26 to August 1 savings event.

Target also said it will host back-to-school events in 2,000 stores on August 8, up from 400 locations last year, and back-to-college move-in events in nearly 150 stores on August 16.

The seasonal fight is not just about price points. Both retailers are bundling value, digital tools, physical events and fulfillment options into one conversion system. For footwear brands, that means the winning partners in peak periods will be the ones that can combine clear entry price architecture with event-ready inventory and content that works online and in store.

Align school-season footwear plans to retailer-specific operating asks: price ladders, fast replenishment, event inventory, app discoverability and localized storytelling. A generic wholesale assortment will underperform in this environment.

Evidence notes

All hard facts above come from primary company materials published on July 15, July 22, July 30 and July 31, 2026. The strategic interpretation is mine. Amazon's AI-usage and Walmart/Target seasonal claims are company-reported and should be treated as directionally useful, not independent proof of downstream profitability.
