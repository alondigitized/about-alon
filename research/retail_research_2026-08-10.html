Retail Intelligence Pulse

Monday, August 10 2026 · Footwear, digital commerce, marketplaces

What newly matters now

August demand is separating into two very different games. In performance footwear, Deckers is still showing that premium brands with product heat can grow faster in direct channels than in wholesale while keeping inventories clean. In mass retail, Walmart and Target are competing for the same back-to-school wallet with loyalty hooks, app-led shopping paths and fast fulfillment. The strategic lesson is that footwear brands should not treat premium growth and value-season traffic as separate topics. They are two ends of the same demand-capture system.

Executive takeaway: plan the next six weeks as a channel-allocation problem, not just a marketing problem. Protect premium DTC where brand heat is real, but assume broader family spending will flow to whichever partner makes value, membership and fulfillment feel easiest.

Deckers is still proving premium footwear can outgrow the market without leaning on broad discounting

Deckers said on July 23, 2026 that first-quarter fiscal 2027 net sales rose 5.7% to $1.02 billion, led by HOKA up 7.7% to $703.5 million and UGG up 4.9% to $278.0 million.

The company said wholesale net sales rose 2.2% to $666.7 million, while direct-to-consumer net sales increased 13.0% to $352.8 million, with DTC comparable net sales up 6.8%.

Gross margin improved to 56.4% from 55.8%, inventories fell to $807.6 million from $849.4 million a year earlier, and full-year sales guidance stayed at $5.86 billion to $5.91 billion even as diluted EPS guidance increased by five cents to $7.35-$7.50.

Deckers Brands Q1 FY2027 results

That mix matters more than the headline growth rate. Deckers is still getting faster growth out of owned digital than wholesale, but management did not raise top-line guidance. That says premium footwear demand is healthy where product and brand are strong, yet management teams still do not trust the broader consumer enough to underwrite aggressive volume assumptions.

Use Deckers as a benchmark for demand quality, not just growth. If your DTC growth is lagging wholesale, or if inventories are rising without a clear product-story reason, you are probably buying volume instead of earning it.

Walmart and Target are showing how the broader August wallet is being captured: membership, convenience and app-led shopping

Walmart said on July 15, 2026 that it is offering its lowest prices since 2019 on 14 popular school supplies and pushing customers toward the Walmart app, classroom supply lists, registries, curbside pickup, fast shipping and Express Delivery in as little as 30 minutes.

Target said on June 2, 2026 that Target Circle Deal Days offered members up to 45% off seasonal items, including 40% off back-to-school and college essentials, with early access for Target Circle 360 members and fulfillment options including same-day delivery, Drive Up and Order Pickup.

NRF said shoppers plan to spend a record $103.5 billion on back-to-school shopping this year, but only 41% plan to shop online, the lowest level since 2016, while department stores and discount stores each reached 33% as shopping destinations.

This is not just promotional noise. Walmart and Target are teaching consumers to solve back-to-school through ecosystems: member pricing, saved lists, pickup, delivery and app-native repeat behavior. At the same time, NRF's destination data says pure online is not taking everything; physical and discount channels still matter. For footwear brands, that means the August fight is less about one hero promotion and more about being easy to discover, compare and receive inside retailer-owned convenience loops.

Audit your back-to-school footwear and apparel execution by partner now: search rank, promo clarity, inventory depth, pickup eligibility, delivery speed and loyalty visibility. If the brand looks good but the shopping path is clumsy, the retailer ecosystem will capture the basket and your share will leak.

Evidence note: Walmart and Target provide strong evidence of current promotional and fulfillment strategy, but they are company communications rather than audited performance outcomes. NRF is useful directional market data, but it is survey-based intent, not transaction data.
