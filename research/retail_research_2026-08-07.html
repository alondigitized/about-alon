Retail Intelligence Pulse

Friday, August 07 2026 · Footwear, digital commerce, marketplaces

What newly matters now

PUMA, Amazon and Target are all signaling the same structural shift from different positions in the stack. Footwear brands are pruning low-quality wholesale, marketplaces are using AI to become stronger discovery layers, and large retailers are trying to pull more of the shopping journey inside conversational interfaces where loyalty, checkout and merchandising can travel together. The fight is no longer just for traffic. It is for who controls the decision path.

Executive takeaway: treat AI shopping surfaces, curated marketplaces and owned-channel economics as one connected agenda. If product data, pricing discipline, loyalty portability and marketplace governance are handled by different teams, you are already behind the change.

PUMA is accepting lower sales to improve channel quality

PUMA said on July 31 that Q2 2026 currency-adjusted sales fell 9.4% to €1.69 billion, but gross margin improved about 180 basis points to 48.0%, inventories fell 15.3% to €1.82 billion and free cash flow rose to €328.8 million from €94.9 million a year earlier.

By channel, wholesale declined 14.0% currency-adjusted while direct-to-consumer rose 0.4%; e-commerce grew 1.8% and DTC mix increased to 35.2% from 32.1%.

PUMA said it continued reducing undesirable wholesale business, especially with mass merchants in North America and Europe, while e-commerce growth was helped by additional marketplaces in APAC and growth in Greater China.

PUMA Q2 2026 release

This is a real reset, not spin. PUMA is explicitly trading top-line volume for healthier distribution, cleaner inventory and better margin structure. For footwear leaders, that matters more than the quarter itself: one of the big global brands is saying publicly that some wholesale revenue is not worth keeping.

Audit which accounts or marketplaces are still generating revenue but degrading margin, brand presentation or forecasting accuracy. If you cannot name them precisely, the clean-up has not started.

Amazon keeps moving from marketplace to AI shopping operating system

Amazon said on July 30 that Q2 2026 net sales increased 20% to $200.6 billion. In the same release, Amazon said active users of Alexa for Shopping were close to doubling and interactions were up more than 5x year over year in Q2.

Amazon also said it launched Amazon Supply Chain Services so outside businesses can use Amazon to move, store and deliver goods, and expanded Ads Agent to 11 new countries, with advertisers using it seeing 8% lower cost per impression and 6% lower cost per acquisition than those that do not.

Amazon said customers who use Alexa for Shopping spend on average more than 40% more per order than customers who do not.

Amazon Q2 2026 earnings release

Amazon is no longer just a place where products are listed and fulfilled. It is building an AI-mediated transaction layer that combines discovery, recommendation, advertising, auto-buy behavior and supply-chain services. That gives Amazon more leverage over how brands are found, compared and ultimately reordered.

Pressure-test your Amazon strategy for an agentic shopping world: richer product attributes, clearer price architecture, stronger ad creative, tighter replenishment logic and explicit rules for when Amazon should drive demand versus when owned channels should.

Evidence note: all numbers above are Amazon-reported. The user, interaction and spend-per-order claims are company disclosures rather than independently verified third-party measurements.

Target is trying to keep discovery, loyalty and checkout together inside AI interfaces

Target said on June 18 that AI-driven traffic to Target jumped 2,000% in Q1 versus a year earlier, versus nearly 400% growth in AI-driven traffic to retail sites overall.

Target said it is the first mass retailer with shopping experiences across Google Search and Gemini, Microsoft Copilot and OpenAI's ChatGPT, with the ability to browse, build baskets and link Target Circle loyalty accounts inside those experiences.

On July 1, Target said Target Plus is expanding with additional brands and that it is working to make marketplace items show up more clearly in AI-powered search and other digital discovery experiences through partners including OpenAI, Google and Microsoft.

Target conversational AI fact sheet
Target Plus marketplace update

Target is making a smart defensive move. If product discovery starts in chat and AI search instead of a retailer homepage, the winning retailers will be the ones that can carry curation, loyalty and fulfillment into that new surface without handing the whole relationship to the platform. That has direct implications for how footwear brands show up in retailer ecosystems.

For key wholesale partners, ask a blunt question: if a consumer starts in ChatGPT, Gemini or Copilot, how does your brand appear, what data travels with it, and where do loyalty, promotions and fulfillment options get decided?

Evidence note: Target's traffic-growth comparison relies on Target's own traffic data and an Adobe benchmark for the broader market. The strategic move is clear; exact market-share implications are still early.
