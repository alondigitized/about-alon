Retail Intelligence Pulse

Monday, August 31 2026 · Footwear, digital commerce, marketplaces

What newly matters now

The cleanest signal heading into September is separation. Premium performance players are still expanding owned retail and protecting a healthier mix, while legacy sneaker channels tied to older silhouettes are still fighting promotions, weaker launches and softer wholesale demand. At the same time, large retailers keep hardening the infrastructure layer around discovery, delivery, checkout and supplier intelligence.

Executive takeaway: keep capital and attention on full-price performance franchises, owned-channel economics and selective distribution. Treat legacy lifestyle inventory and wholesale-dependent exposure as a risk-management problem, not a demand problem you can market your way out of.

On is still pressing owned retail where performance demand is strongest

On said on August 28 that it opened its first Dallas store at NorthPark Center, a roughly 3,000-square-foot location carrying running, tennis, training, outdoor and lifestyle assortments. The company said the Dallas opening brings it to 17 stores in the U.S. and more than 70 worldwide.

On opens Dallas NorthPark Center store (August 28, 2026)

This is not just store-count vanity. On is still using owned retail to deepen premium brand control in influential U.S. markets while performance running remains one of the healthiest pockets in footwear demand.

Watch where premium run brands choose to place owned stores. Those locations are effectively live maps of where they see enough brand pull, margin and community density to justify tighter control of the consumer relationship.

The healthier footwear operators are still winning on mix and channel discipline

Crocs said on July 30 that its Crocs brand DTC revenue rose 12.9% in Q2 2026 while wholesale fell 5.0%; HEYDUDE DTC rose 7.2% while wholesale fell 17.2%. Separately, Reuters reported on July 23 that Deckers' first-quarter DTC net sales increased 8.4%, HOKA sales rose 7.7%, and the company raised its fiscal 2027 earnings outlook.

Reuters on Deckers Q1 FY2027 results (July 23, 2026)

The pattern is pretty clear: brands with product momentum and tighter channel control are still finding growth in DTC and selective distribution, even in a choppy consumer environment. That matters because it protects both pricing power and customer data quality.

Score major footwear businesses on two axes: product heat and channel control. Brands that lack both are the ones most likely to be forced into discounting and dependency on third-party clearance behavior.

Legacy sneaker distribution is still stuck in a promotional trap

JD Sports said on August 20 that North America like-for-like sales fell 6.8% in Q2 and that footwear remained soft amid consumer pressure, product-cycle weakness and a promotional market, even as online sales grew 2.6% with support from store-based fulfillment. Reuters then reported on August 25 that DICK'S Sporting Goods cut guidance and said bloated inventory and heavy discounting in legacy footwear were hurting Foot Locker.

JD Sports Q2 2026/27 trading statement (August 20, 2026)
Reuters on DICK'S / Foot Locker (August 25, 2026)

This is the dangerous part of the cycle for wholesale-heavy footwear players: fewer must-have launches, aging lifestyle franchises and too much inventory create a markdown loop that gets worse as channel partners chase volume.

Separate wholesale partners by their exposure to fresh performance product versus aging lifestyle inventory. The same channel strategy should not apply to both, and the wrong one will cost margin fast.

Target and Walmart keep strengthening the retail operating system underneath the shopper journey

Target said on August 19 that digital comparable sales grew 8.7%, driven by more than 25% growth in same-day delivery, while non-merchandise sales rose more than 20% on strength in Roundel, Target Circle 360 and Target Plus; it also said digital traffic from external AI platforms is growing more than 3.5 times the industry versus a year ago. Walmart said on August 21 that Tap to Pay would begin rolling out from August 24, and Walmart Data Ventures said on August 19 that Scintilla added AI-generated customer-perception summaries plus richer digital-behavior and benchmark tools for suppliers.

The biggest retailers are widening their moat below the surface: AI traffic capture, same-day fulfillment, easier checkout and better supplier data are converging into one operating advantage. That is harder to copy than a campaign or a chatbot demo.

Benchmark your digital stack against this combined standard: discovery in AI surfaces, conversion on owned properties, frictionless payment, fast fulfillment and actionable shared data with key partners. Weakness in any one layer now leaks value across the whole system.
