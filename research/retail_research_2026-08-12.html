Retail Intelligence Pulse

Wednesday, August 12 2026 · Footwear, digital commerce, marketplaces

What newly matters now

Three signals are lining up. First, On’s August 11 print showed premium performance footwear can still compound growth while increasing DTC mix and gross margin. Second, Crocs’ late-July results showed even a bigger, more mature footwear platform can still push double-digit DTC growth while leaning less on wholesale. Third, PUMA’s newly created e-commerce leadership role is a tell that weaker players increasingly see digital operating model, not just product, as the battleground. The real shift is that footwear winners are not merely selling more shoes; they are taking more control over where discovery happens, how price is held, and which channel captures the margin.

Executive takeaway: treat channel architecture as a strategic weapon. The brands outperforming now are protecting full-price demand, raising DTC productivity and tightening digital ownership at the same time.

On is still the clearest fresh proof point that premium footwear growth can coexist with margin expansion

On reported on August 11, 2026 that second-quarter net sales rose 13.5% year over year to CHF 850.3 million, or 21.6% on a constant-currency basis.

Direct-to-consumer sales increased 26.0% to CHF 388.4 million, while wholesale increased 4.8% to CHF 461.9 million. DTC reached 45.7% of net sales.

Gross margin expanded to 65.4% from 61.5%, and the company raised its full-year gross margin expectation to at least 65.0% while maintaining low-20% constant-currency sales growth guidance.

On Q2 2026 results

This is the strongest current read on what "healthy" looks like in performance footwear: brand heat, controlled wholesale, rising owned-channel mix and enough pricing power to offset tariff pressure. The bigger point is not that On is growing fast. It is that On is doing it without obvious promotional compromise.

Use On as the benchmark for channel quality, not just growth. Ask whether your mix shift is coming from real consumer pull and better DTC productivity, or from wholesale expansion and markdown support.

Crocs is showing a larger footwear platform can still migrate profitably toward DTC

Crocs said on July 30, 2026 that second-quarter consolidated revenues increased 2.6% to $1.179 billion.

Direct-to-consumer revenues grew 12.0%, while wholesale revenues declined 7.2%.

The Crocs brand surpassed $1 billion in quarterly revenue for the first time, and the company raised full-year 2026 guidance for both revenue and adjusted diluted EPS.

Crocs Q2 2026 results

Crocs matters here because it broadens the lesson beyond premium running. Even in a more mature and more value-exposed footwear model, management is getting more growth from DTC than wholesale. That suggests the industry’s margin pool is still shifting toward brands that control consumer data, merchandising cadence and conversion surfaces directly.

Pressure-test whether your DTC business is genuinely incremental or just absorbing volume that wholesale used to do more efficiently. The Crocs signal is useful because the model is large enough to show the shift can work at scale.

PUMA’s new e-commerce leadership role is an operating-model signal, not a routine personnel move

PUMA announced on July 22, 2026 that it appointed Dusan Hamlin to the newly created role of Vice President E-Commerce, reporting to Chief Commercial Officer Matthias Bäumer.

The company said the move follows its late-2025 decision to split direct-to-consumer into two dedicated areas: Global Retail and Global E-Commerce.

PUMA said Hamlin will drive global digital and e-commerce strategy, and explicitly cited website, app and marketplace consumer experience as focus areas.

PUMA appoints Vice President E-Commerce

This is what catch-up looks like when a brand believes its digital stack and operating model need sharper ownership. The important takeaway is not the hire itself. It is that PUMA is formalizing e-commerce as a standalone leadership domain, which usually means the old blended DTC model was not moving fast enough across site, app, marketplaces and digital experience.

If digital commerce still sits as a sub-function inside a broader retail org, revisit whether you actually have enough dedicated leadership to move fast on personalization, search, merchandising, marketplaces and post-purchase experience.
