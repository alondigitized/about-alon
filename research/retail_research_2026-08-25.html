Retail Intelligence Pulse

Tuesday, August 25 2026 · Footwear, digital commerce, marketplaces

What newly matters now

No major core-footwear brand reset the picture overnight. The more important update by Tuesday, August 25 is that last week’s signals now line up: U.S. e-commerce is growing materially faster than total retail again, big-box retailers are converting that traffic with tighter AI, loyalty and delivery systems, and multi-brand footwear demand is still soft enough that weak execution gets exposed fast. That combination favors the retailers and brands that can win discovery, trust inventory promises and remove friction end to end.

Executive takeaway: assume channel control is getting more valuable than broad distribution. Prioritize the accounts and surfaces where your product is easiest to find, easiest to buy and easiest to fulfill profitably.

U.S. e-commerce is pulling away from total retail again

The U.S. Census Bureau said second-quarter 2026 retail e-commerce sales were $340.2 billion, up 3.8% sequentially and 12.2% year over year, while total retail sales rose 2.9% sequentially and 6.7% year over year. E-commerce represented 17.1% of total retail sales in the quarter.

U.S. Census Bureau: Quarterly Retail E-Commerce Sales, Q2 2026

That is the cleanest current demand-shape signal in the market. Online is not merely holding share; it is expanding faster than retail overall. For footwear, that means digital shelf quality, paid visibility, recommendation readiness and fulfillment reliability matter even more because more demand is flowing through algorithmic and convenience-led journeys.

Re-rank digital priorities around conversion leverage: search content, inventory accuracy, local availability, delivery promise and retail-media support on hero franchises should all move ahead of lower-signal brand activity.

Target is treating AI, loyalty and marketplace growth as one operating system

Target said Q2 2026 net sales rose 5.3%, comparable sales rose 3.8%, store comp sales rose 2.7% and digital comp sales rose 8.7%, led by more than 25% growth in same-day delivery. It also said non-merchandise sales grew more than 20%, reflecting growth in Roundel ad revenue, Target Circle 360 membership revenue and the Target+ marketplace.

Target also said digital traffic from external AI platforms like OpenAI and Google is growing more than 3.5x the industry versus a year ago, that AI-powered Teacher and College Wish List recommendations are driving digital conversion, and that Chandhu Nair joined on August 24 as Target’s first chief AI officer.

This is not generic AI theater. Target is explicitly linking AI-driven traffic, recommendation-led conversion, membership economics, marketplace assortment and same-day fulfillment. That is the model to pay attention to: the retailer that best connects discovery signals to inventory confidence will capture more demand and shape brand visibility.

For wholesale and DTC teams, stop treating retail media, CRM, search content and fulfillment readiness as separate programs. Retail partners are increasingly evaluating them as one performance stack.

JD Sports is still the clearest footwear warning: demand is selective and promotional

JD Sports said Q2 North America organic sales fell 4.5% and like-for-like sales fell 6.8%. The company said footwear remained soft in a promotional market, sales reflected weaker core consumer sentiment, and North America was hurt by a slower quarter for high-heat footwear product and deferred back-to-school demand.

JD Sports Q2 2026/27 trading statement

JD also said group online sales grew 2.6%, supported by strength in apparel and store-based fulfillment, while momentum was better in performance-based running and newer footwear styles than in end-of-cycle footwear lines.

JD Sports Q2 2026/27 trading statement

That is the clearest current read-through for multi-brand footwear: digital capability helps, but it does not rescue tired product cycles or overly broad promotional exposure. Premium performance running and newerness are holding up better than older footwear lines, while weak demand is punishing undifferentiated assortment.

Tighten the line plan. Back franchises with fresh product energy and proven conversion support; be more ruthless on end-of-cycle exposure, promotional dependency and weak account-level productivity.

Evidence notes

The strongest facts here come from primary sources: the U.S. Census Bureau, Target’s own earnings and corporate disclosures, and JD Sports’ regulatory statement. The interpretation is that these sources together show a widening gap between strong digital operating models and soft multi-brand footwear demand. No major new overnight disclosure from Nike, adidas, Deckers, Crocs, On, HOKA or Puma materially changed that view by August 25, so this brief stays narrow on the signals that actually moved the decision picture.
